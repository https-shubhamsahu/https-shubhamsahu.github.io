NO SUS

A note or file that opens once. Then it's gone.

NO SUS: Self-Destructing Notes is an Android and web app. Send a note or file by a one-time link or a 6-digit code; the reader opens it once in any browser, with no account, and the encrypted copy is deleted from the server. Two tabs, Chat and Send.

Live on Google Play (1.1.11) + web

What I built

Sharing a file often means losing track of access. I wanted the sender to keep useful controls after sending it.

  • Built a Flutter client and Supabase backend. Notes and files are encrypted on the phone and the link carries the key; the link first shows a button, so a chat app’s link preview cannot use it up.
  • Send three ways: one open (gone after the first open), watermarked (the email the reader enters is printed on every page, and you can revoke the link) and traceable copies (notes only; a leak check shows whose copy leaked, a strong hint, not proof).
  • Sent links show Opened or Not opened: you see when, never who. The reader needs no account and no app.
  • Open your chat on any computer by matching a number on the phone; that screen never gets the Google password. Chat stays on the phone, or in your own Google Drive if you connect it.
  • Watermarks and leak checks give an attribution clue, not verified identity. Downloaded files and copied text can stay on the reader’s device. Android 1.1.11 is on Google Play; screenshots are blocked only inside the Android app.

Tools: Flutter, Dart, Supabase, Browser cryptography.
